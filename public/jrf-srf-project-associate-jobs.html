Frequently Asked Questions

Which organisations advertise JRF and SRF positions in India?
JRF and SRF positions in environment, forestry, ecology and climate disciplines are advertised by Central, State and Agricultural universities, IITs, IISc, CSIR and ICAR laboratories, ICFRE institutes, the Wildlife Institute of India, IIFM, ATREE and project-mode research programmes funded by DST, DBT, MoEFCC and similar agencies.
What is the eligibility for JRF and SRF roles?
A JRF typically requires an M.Sc. / M.Tech. in a relevant discipline with UGC-NET or GATE qualification, while an SRF requires the same plus roughly two years of research experience. Individual advertisements specify exact subject requirements — always check the official notification linked from each listing.
What stipend do JRF / SRF and project associate positions pay?
Stipends follow funding-agency norms — commonly around Rs 37,000–42,000 per month for JRF and Rs 42,000–46,000 for SRF under national fellowship schemes — while project associate positions at institutes and IITs follow the DST pay matrix (about Rs 25,000–56,000 depending on level). The registry records the compensation stated in each advertisement.
How often are new research vacancies added?
The registry refreshes every day at 7 AM IST: official institute portals, verified job feeds and news alerts for JRF, SRF and project associate advertisements are scanned automatically, so new openings usually appear within 24 hours of publication.
What is the difference between JRF jobs, SRF vacancies and project associate posts?
JRF jobs are entry research fellowships, usually requiring UGC-NET or GATE. SRF vacancies require about two years of research experience. Project associate and research associate posts are project-funded roles with institute-specific pay.
