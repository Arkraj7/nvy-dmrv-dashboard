How to Crack CSIR NET Life Sciences (2026)

Published 29 October 2026 · By Arkraj Biswas, Urban Forest Park Editorial

CSIR-UGC NET Life Sciences is the gateway to research fellowships and lectureship positions across India. Over 2 lakh candidates appear each cycle, but only ~6% qualify for JRF. This guide breaks down the exam, syllabus and a realistic preparation strategy.

1. Exam pattern

CSIR NET Life Sciences has three parts:

  • Part A (General Aptitude): 20 questions, 15 to attempt, 30 marks. Logical reasoning, numerical ability, data interpretation.
  • Part B (Subject-specific): 50 questions, 35 to attempt, 70 marks. Core life sciences topics.
  • Part C (Higher-order): 75 questions, 25 to attempt, 100 marks. Application-based, scenario-driven questions.

Total: 150 questions, 75 to attempt, 200 marks, 3 hours. Negative marking: 25% for Part B and C.

3. 6-month study plan

  1. Months 1–2: Units 2–7 (core biology). Focus on molecular biology, cell biology, physiology and genetics. These form the bulk of Part B.
  2. Months 3–4: Units 8–12 (ecology, evolution, applied). Ecology and evolution are high-scoring and overlap with forestry/environmental science.
  3. Month 5: Part A + previous-year papers. Solve 10 years of CSIR NET papers. Master the aptitude section — it's the easiest 30 marks.
  4. Month 6: Mock tests + weak areas. Take full-length mocks weekly. Identify and fix weak topics. Revise high-weightage units.

5. JRF vs LS: which to target?

  • JRF (Junior Research Fellowship): Qualifies you for a research fellowship at CSIR labs, IITs, IISc and universities. Requires a higher cutoff. Age limit 28 (relaxable).
  • LS (Lectureship): Qualifies you for assistant professor positions. Lower cutoff than JRF. No age limit.
  • Strategy: Prepare for JRF-level difficulty. If you clear JRF, you automatically qualify for LS. If you miss JRF but clear LS, you still have a lectureship.

Frequently asked questions

Is CSIR NET Life Sciences enough for a JRF at IIT?

IITs may require additional institute-specific tests or interviews. A valid CSIR NET JRF score is a strong advantage but not always sufficient on its own.

Can I prepare for CSIR NET while working full-time?

Yes, but it requires disciplined time management. Dedicate 2–3 hours on weekdays and 6–8 hours on weekends. Focus on high-weightage topics and previous-year papers.

What is the pass rate for CSIR NET Life Sciences?

Approximately 6% qualify for JRF and 12–15% for LS. The exam is competitive but the cutoff is achievable with consistent preparation.
