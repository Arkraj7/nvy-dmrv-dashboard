How to Prepare for JRF in Forestry & Environment (India 2026)

Published 28 October 2026 · Urban Forest Park Editorial

India's forestry and environmental research sector offers one of the most structured fellowship pathways for postgraduates. The Junior Research Fellowship (JRF) is the entry point: a monthly stipend plus contingency grant that funds 2 years of research, extendable to a PhD. This guide covers everything from eligibility to exam strategy.

1. What is JRF in forestry and environment?

A JRF is a research stipend awarded to candidates who clear national or institute-level entrance exams. In the green sector, JRF holders work on projects at ICAR institutes, IITs, IISc, the Wildlife Institute of India (WII), ICFRE, ATREE, CEEW and state universities. The stipend is typically ₹37,000/month (JRF) rising to ₹42,000/month (SRF) plus HRA.

2. Which exams should you target?

  • CSIR-UGC NET — Life Sciences and Earth, Atmospheric, Ocean & Planetary Sciences papers. Qualifies you for JRF at CSIR labs and UGC-funded universities.
  • ICAR AIEEA (PG) — for fellowships at ICAR institutes (IFGTB, ICFRE, IARI, etc.).
  • GATE — Ecology & Evolution, Life Sciences, or Environmental Science & Engineering papers. IITs and IISc use GATE scores for PhD admission with fellowship.
  • Institute-specific tests — WII, ICFRE, ATREE and several central universities conduct their own entrance exams with JRF attached.

3. Eligibility checklist

  • Master's degree in a relevant subject (Forestry, Ecology, Botany, Zoology, Environmental Science, Geography, Agriculture, etc.) with ≥ 55% marks (50% for SC/ST/OBC/PwD).
  • Age limit: ≤ 28 years for JRF (relaxable for reserved categories and women).
  • Some institutes require a valid NET/GATE score; others accept their own test.

4. 6-month preparation strategy

  1. Months 1–2: Core subjects. Cover ecology, taxonomy, silviculture, forest management, biostatistics, and environmental chemistry from standard textbooks.
  2. Months 3–4: Applied topics. Remote sensing, GIS, climate change, biodiversity conservation, EIA, and environmental policy.
  3. Month 5: Previous-year papers. Solve at least 5 years of CSIR NET and ICAR AIEEA papers under timed conditions.
  4. Month 6: Mock tests + revision. Take weekly full-length mocks; revise weak areas; focus on high-weightage topics.

5. Recommended resources

  • Ecology: Concepts and Applications — Molles
  • Forest Ecology — Barnes, Zak, Denton & Spurr
  • Environmental Science — Benny Joseph or Ravi Krishnan
  • Biostatistics — Zar or Sokal & Rohlf (selected chapters)
  • CSIR NET previous-year papers (available on the CSIR website)
  • ICAR AIEEA sample papers

7. Application tips

  • Apply early — many institutes close applications 2–3 weeks before the exam.
  • Keep scanned copies of your degree, caste certificate (if applicable) and photo ready.
  • Check the institute's website for the exact syllabus; it varies between CSIR, ICAR and institute-specific tests.

Frequently asked questions

What is the JRF stipend in 2026?

The JRF stipend is ₹37,000/month plus HRA and contingency grant. After 2 years it upgrades to SRF at ₹42,000/month.

Can I apply for JRF after M.Sc.?

Yes. A Master's degree with ≥ 55% marks is the minimum qualification. Some institutes also accept final-year M.Sc. students.

Is GATE mandatory for JRF?

No. CSIR-UGC NET and ICAR AIEEA are the primary routes. GATE is used by IITs and IISc for their own fellowships.
