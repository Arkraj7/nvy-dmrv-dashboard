Climate Fellowships in India 2026: Eligibility & Application Guide

Published 28 October 2026 · Urban Forest Park Editorial

Climate fellowships are the bridge between academic training and a career in sustainability. They offer funding, mentorship and a professional network that can accelerate your trajectory. India has a growing ecosystem of climate fellowships — from government-backed programmes to corporate and NGO initiatives. This guide covers the most relevant ones for 2026.

1. Types of climate fellowships

  • Research fellowships: Fund a specific research project (PhD or postdoc). Examples: IIT/IISc PhD fellowships, TERI research fellowships, ATREE PhD programmes.
  • Leadership fellowships: Build capacity in young professionals through cohort-based programmes. Examples: Climate Leadership Programme (ClimAct India), GEAF Ambassador Programme, European Climate Pact Ambassador.
  • Policy fellowships: Place fellows in government or think-tank roles. Examples: CEEW fellowships, WRI India research fellowships.
  • International fellowships: Open to Indian applicants for study/research abroad. Examples: Chevening, Fulbright, Erasmus Mundus, MOPGA.

3. How to choose the right fellowship

  • Match your stage: Undergraduate → leadership/ambassador programmes; Master's → research fellowships and international scholarships; PhD → research fellowships; Professional → policy fellowships.
  • Check funding: Some fellowships are fully funded (tuition + stipend + travel); others are volunteer-based with networking benefits only.
  • Consider geography: India-based fellowships keep you close to local climate issues; international ones build global networks.
  • Look at alumni outcomes: Where do past fellows work? This predicts your trajectory.

4. Application strategy

  1. Start 3 months early. Most fellowships require a research proposal, personal statement and 2–3 recommendation letters.
  2. Tailor your proposal. Align with the fellowship's stated themes (climate adaptation, mitigation, policy, biodiversity, etc.).
  3. Secure a mentor. For research fellowships, a faculty sponsor or host institution dramatically improves your chances.
  4. Highlight impact. Fellows are selected for potential impact, not just grades. Show how your work will make a difference.
  5. Proofread and peer-review. Have 2–3 people review your application before submission.

5. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying to fellowships outside your eligibility (age, nationality, degree level).
  • Generic personal statements — tailor each application.
  • Missing deadlines due to time-zone confusion (common for international fellowships).
  • Not checking whether the fellowship requires a host institution before applying.

Frequently asked questions

Are climate fellowships paid?

Research and policy fellowships (CEEW, TERI, Harvard, MOPGA, Chevening, Fulbright) are fully funded. Leadership and ambassador programmes (ClimAct, GEAF, European Climate Pact) are typically volunteer-based with networking benefits.

Can I apply for multiple fellowships simultaneously?

Yes, but check each programme's exclusivity clause. Some require you to decline other funded offers if selected.

Do fellowships require a research proposal?

Research fellowships (MOPGA, Harvard, MITTAL) require a detailed proposal. Leadership programmes usually require a personal statement and project idea. International scholarships (Chevening, Erasmus) require essays and a study plan.
