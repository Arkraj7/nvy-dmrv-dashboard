What is a JRF? Junior Research Fellow Explained

A Junior Research Fellow is a funded research position attached to a project or a PhD programme. The fellow does research, fieldwork and analysis under a principal investigator and receives a monthly fellowship rather than a salary.

Why it matters

This is where the term shows up in practice and in hiring.

  • Eligibility usually requires a master's degree plus a national eligibility test such as CSIR-UGC NET, UGC-NET or GATE.
  • Fellowship amounts follow the funding agency's norms and are printed in each advertisement.
  • After PhD registration the fellow is usually upgraded to Senior Research Fellow.
  • JRF experience counts as research experience for later academic and scientist posts.

The definition and rules above come from the body that owns the term. This site does not paraphrase a regulation into a simpler claim than the source supports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is JRF a job?
It is a fellowship, not employment. It carries a stipend and usually requires PhD registration to continue.
Can I take a JRF without a PhD seat?
Yes for the initial period. Most institutes require registration before upgrading to SRF.
Is the JRF stipend taxable?
Fellowships are generally treated differently from salary. Confirm with the institute and a tax adviser.
