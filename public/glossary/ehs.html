What is EHS? Environment, Health and Safety Explained

EHS stands for Environment, Health and Safety. It is the combined function in an organisation responsible for environmental compliance, worker safety and occupational health.

Why it matters

This is where the term shows up in practice and in hiring.

  • EHS roles sit at the intersection of pollution control, safety management and compliance.
  • They are common in manufacturing, energy, infrastructure and chemicals.
  • Entry usually requires an engineering degree plus knowledge of environmental law and safety standards.
  • EHS officers often move into sustainability and ESG roles later in their careers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is EHS the same as ESG?
No. EHS is operational compliance and safety. ESG is broader reporting and governance. They overlap in practice.
Do EHS roles need a safety certification?
Some employers ask for NEBOSH or IOSH, but many hire on engineering degree and plant experience.
Is EHS work field-based?
Largely yes. It involves site inspections, audits and training rather than desk reporting.
