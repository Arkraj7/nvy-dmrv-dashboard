What is a Carbon Credit? Carbon Markets Explained

A carbon credit is a tradable certificate representing one tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent that has been reduced, avoided or removed from the atmosphere. Carbon markets let emitters buy credits to offset emissions they cannot yet eliminate.

Why it matters

This is where the term shows up in practice and in hiring.

  • Voluntary carbon markets trade credits from projects such as forestry, renewables and cookstoves.
  • Compliance markets are created by regulation, such as the EU Emissions Trading System.
  • India is developing its own carbon credit trading scheme, which is creating demand for MRV and carbon accounting skills.
  • Jobs in this area include carbon analysts, project developers, verifiers and registry staff.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a carbon credit the same as a carbon offset?
In practice the terms are used interchangeably, though 'offset' describes the use and 'credit' the unit.
Are carbon credits controversial?
Yes. Quality, additionality and double counting are debated. Good projects publish their methodology and verification.
Can individuals buy carbon credits?
Yes, through voluntary market retailers, though the impact depends on the project quality.
