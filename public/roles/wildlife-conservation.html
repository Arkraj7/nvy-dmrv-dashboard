In short

A wildlife biologist surveys species and habitats and advises protected-area managers. A master's in wildlife science or ecology is the common entry point.

How people enter

Drawn from the qualification and experience requirements in the current listings.

  • A master's in wildlife science, zoology, ecology or environmental science is the common entry point.
  • Field experience is decisive: a season of survey work outweighs a classroom certificate.
  • Occupancy modelling, distance sampling and R are named in most research listings.
  • The WII postgraduate diploma is one recognised route into the sector.

How to benchmark when nothing is published

Most Indian notices leave pay out. These questions get you a comparable answer without a guessed band.

  • Ask for the fixed and variable split. A headline CTC that is 20% variable is not the same as a fixed package.
  • Ask what the band is for the grade. Companies that will not quote a number will usually confirm a band once you are shortlisted.
  • Compare the whole offer. Provident fund, gratuity, insurance, travel and field allowance change the effective value materially in this sector.
  • Check the contract type. Fellowship, project contract and permanent posts are taxed and renewed differently; the notice states which one applies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to be physically fit?
Field roles require it. Surveys often run for weeks in remote areas, and the interview will probe your field experience.
Is conservation work well paid?
The registry has too few disclosed figures to generalise. Government and institute posts follow pay norms; NGO roles vary widely.
Which skills get shortlisted?
Species identification, camera-trap handling, occupancy or abundance modelling, and GIS.
